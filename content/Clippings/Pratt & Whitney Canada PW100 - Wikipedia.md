The **Pratt & Whitney Canada PW100** [aircraft engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine "Aircraft engine") family is a series of 1,800 to 5,000 [shaft horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower#Shaft_horsepower "Horsepower") (1,300 to 3,700 kW) [turboprops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop "Turboprop") manufactured by [Pratt & Whitney Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney_Canada "Pratt & Whitney Canada"). Pratt & Whitney Canada dominates the turboprop market with 89% of the turboprop [regional airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airliner "Regional airliner") installed base in 2016, leading [GE Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Aviation "GE Aviation") and [Allison Engine Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Engine_Company "Allison Engine Company").[^4]

## Development

The engine was first introduced as a technology demonstrator in 1977.[^5] The PW100 was first tested in March 1981, made its initial flight in February 1982 on a [Vickers Viscount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Viscount "Vickers Viscount") [testbed aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testbed_aircraft "Testbed aircraft"),[^6] and then entered service in December 1984 on a [Dash 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_8 "Dash 8") [regional aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_aircraft "Regional aircraft") for [NorOntair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NorOntair "NorOntair").[^3]

The PW150 engine was introduced on 24 April 1995, when [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Inc. "Bombardier Inc.") selected the engine for the launch of its de Havilland Dash 8-400 regional turboprop. The PW150 was a higher-power version of the PW100 series, with the low-pressure compressor changed from a single-stage [centrifugal compressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_compressor "Centrifugal compressor") to a three-stage [axial compressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_compressor "Axial compressor"), and the turbine modified to have improved cooling. The power rating was increased from 2,750 shaft horsepower (2,050 kilowatts) in the PW127 to 4,920 shp (3,670 kW) in the PW150, although the engine was thermodynamically capable of 6,500–7,500 shp (4,800–5,600 kW).[^7]

At the 2021 [Dubai Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Air_Show "Dubai Air Show"), Pratt & Whitney Canada introduced the PW127XT (extended-time-on-wing) series. The PW127XT, which is intended to replace the PW127M variant, reduces the number of engine overhauls within 10 years to two from three. The engine maintenance interval (time-on-wing) is increased from 14,000 hours to 20,000 hours and would use three percent less fuel than the PW127M.[^8] The engine series will premiere as the standard powerplant on all new [ATR 42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_42 "ATR 42") and [ATR 72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72 "ATR 72") aircraft, with a launch order from [Air Corsica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Corsica "Air Corsica") using the PW127XT-M engine model. The PW127XT-N variant, which is designed for the ATR 72-600, has the same mechanical power rating as the PW127XT-M but has a higher thermodynamic power rating.[^9]

## Design

Originally called the PT7, the PW100 uses a relatively unusual three-shaft engine configuration. In the PW100, a centrifugal low-pressure (LP) [impeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeller "Impeller") (except for the PW150, which uses a 3-stage axial LP compressor), driven by a single-stage LP [turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine "Turbine"), supercharges a [contra-rotating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contra-rotating "Contra-rotating") centrifugal high-pressure (HP) impeller, driven by a single-stage HP turbine. Power is delivered to the offset propeller [reduction gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_gearbox "Reduction gearbox") through a third shaft, connected to a two-stage [free (power) turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-turbine_turboshaft "Free-turbine turboshaft"). The gearbox has two stages, yielding a reduction ratio between 15.4 and 17.16. The first stage uses [double helical gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear#Double_helical "Gear"), followed by a second stage with straight [spur gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_gear "Spur gear").[^10]

2,500 shp (1,900 kW) turboshaft version that powered the first 2 prototypes of the [Mil Mi-38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-38 "Mil Mi-38") helicopter [^18] and was to be used on the Mi-38-1 variant.[^19]

Selected to power the [Deutsche Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Aircraft "Deutsche Aircraft") [D328eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D328eco "D328eco") in June 2022.[^20]

PW130

Unsuccessfully offered for the [Saab 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_2000 "Saab 2000") [^21] and [IPTN N-250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPTN_N-250 "IPTN N-250") aircraft.[^22] Proposed for the unbuilt [Fokker 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_50 "Fokker 50") -400 aircraft.[^23]

PW150 Twin Pack

Proposed powerplant for the [Airbus A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A400M "Airbus A400M").[^24] Two PW150-based engines would be used to drive a single propeller.[^25] The powerplant was eliminated from contention by Airbus in early June 1999, since it was short of providing the 9,000 shp (6,700 kW) required to drive the eight-bladed propeller at the time, and its specific fuel consumption (SFC) was excessive.[^26]

PW150B

Proposed powerplant for the [Shaanxi Y-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi_Y-8 "Shaanxi Y-8") F-600.[^27] Abandoned in December 2008 when the United States government barred a U.S.-based subcontractor from exporting the engine control software for the PW150B.[^28]

PW150C

Proposed powerplant for the [Xian MA700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian_MA700 "Xian MA700").[^29] Includes a third-stage power turbine, larger-diameter propellers, modified [reduction gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_gearbox "Reduction gearbox"), and optimized low-pressure compressor.[^30] Has higher thrust, higher speed, and extended range compared to the PW150A. Blocked from an export license by the Canadian government in 2020,[^31] because of the Chinese government's retaliatory detention of Canadian citizens (the " [two Michaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Michaels "Two Michaels") ") starting in 2018.[^32]

ST18M

Marine application for the PW100.

ST40

PW150 derivative adapted for the [Bombardier JetTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_JetTrain "Bombardier JetTrain"), which was proposed for use in high-speed train travel in North America.[^33]

ST40M

Marine application for the PW150A.

#### General characteristics

- Type: Three-shaft turboprop
- Length: 2,046–2,130 mm (80.6–83.9 in); _PW150:_ 2,420 mm (95 in)
- Diameter: 635–679 mm (25.0–26.7 in); _PW150:_ 790 mm (31 in)
- Dry weight: 390.5–481.7 kg (861–1,062 lb); _PW150:_ 716.9 kg (1,580 lb)

#### Components

- Compressor: Two-spool, single-stage centrifugal LP + single-stage centrifugal HP

_PW150:_ Two-spool, three-stage axial LP + single-stage centrifugal HP [^35]

- [Combustors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustor "Combustor"): Reverse flow combustor [^35]
- [Turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine "Turbine"): Single-stage HP and LP turbines, two-stage power turbine [^35]
- Fuel type: _PW150:_ Kerosene Jet A, A-1/JP8; Wide Cut Jet B/JP4; High Flash JP5/JP1
- Oil system: Self-contained system [^40]

#### Performance

- Maximum power output: 1,342–1,846 kW (1,800–2,476 hp); _PW150:_ 3,415 kW (4,580 hp) + 3.412 kN (767 lbf)
- [Overall pressure ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overall_pressure_ratio "Overall pressure ratio"): _PW120, PW127, and PW150:_ 12.14, 15.77, and 17.97 [^10]
- Air mass flow: _PW120, PW127, and PW150:_ 6.70, 8.49, and 14.44 kg/s (14.8, 18.7, and 31.8 lb/s) [^10]
- [Power-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-weight_ratio "Power-to-weight ratio"): 3.44–3.83 kW/kg (2.09–2.33 hp/lb); _PW150:_ 4.76 kW/kg (2.90 hp/lb)
